Why does the New Testament often use "He" when referring to the Holy Spirit? Part 7 looks closely at the Greek word **paracletos** — Comforter, Helper, Advocate — and explains why masculine Greek pronouns follow it in John 14–16.

The episode compares the neuter Greek *pneuma*, used for the Spirit throughout most of the New Testament, with the feminine Hebrew *ruah*, used for the Spirit more than a hundred times in the Old Testament. Yona Tree's position is that grammatical gender alone does not settle the question either way; what matters most is the nature and role of the Spirit and the comparisons Scripture makes to Her.

It also revisits *gennao* and the distinction between begetting and birth, and returns to Eden: Eve was made a "help meet" — a helper suitable — for Adam, and the Spirit is the Paraclete, the Helper of the redeemed.
