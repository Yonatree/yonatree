In Galatians 4, Paul tells the story of two mothers in Abraham's house and declares that it means more than history:

> Which things are an allegory: for these are the two covenants; the one from the mount Sinai, which gendereth to bondage, which is Agar… and answereth to Jerusalem which now is, and is in bondage with her children. But Jerusalem which is above is free, which is the mother of us all.
>
> — Galatians 4:24–26

## Two mothers

**Hagar** was a bondwoman, a servant in Abraham's house. **Sarah** was a free woman, Abraham's wife, and the mother of the child of promise, Isaac. Paul builds his allegory on the difference between them.

> Now we, brethren, as Isaac was, are the children of promise. But as then he that was born after the flesh persecuted him that was born after the Spirit, even so it is now… So then, brethren, we are not children of the bondwoman, but of the free.
>
> — Galatians 4:28–31

## Children of promise

The Holy Spirit is called "that holy Spirit of promise" (Ephesians 1:13), and those born of the Spirit are "the children of the promise" (Romans 9:8). Isaac was a child of promise born to a woman who could not have children by natural means. Believers, Paul says, are children of promise in the same way: born not merely according to the flesh but by the power of God.

:::yonatree
Sarah represents the Spirit of promise, and Hagar our fleshly mothers who bear us into a world under the bondage of sin. When we are reborn into the Kingdom of God, we gain not only a Heavenly Father but the promise of the Holy Spirit — the ultimate Free Woman, of whom Sarah was a figure. That freedom was purchased by the precious blood of Jesus Christ.
:::

## Two mountains

The letter to the Hebrews describes the same contrast in terms of two mountains:

> For ye are not come unto the mount that might be touched, and that burned with fire… But ye are come unto mount Sion, and unto the city of the living God, the heavenly Jerusalem, and to an innumerable company of angels, to the general assembly and church of the firstborn, which are written in heaven.
>
> — Hebrews 12:18, 22–23

It was in Jerusalem, at Pentecost, that the promised Spirit was poured out "as of a rushing mighty wind" (Acts 2:2). No one needs to travel to the physical Mount Zion to receive that gift. We may come to the Father in Jesus' name and ask for the Holy Spirit to dwell in us.

## An elder and a younger Jerusalem

Scripture speaks of the Jerusalem which is above *now* — and of a *New* Jerusalem still to come, "coming down from God out of heaven, prepared as a bride adorned for her husband" (Revelation 21:2). An angel shows John "the bride, the Lamb's wife," and what he sees is the holy city (Revelation 21:9–10).

:::scripture
Paul calls the present Jerusalem above "the mother of us all" (Galatians 4:26). John calls the coming New Jerusalem the Bride (Revelation 21:2, 9–10). Both are female figures connected with the people of God.
:::

:::yonatree
The present heavenly Jerusalem is pictured as a mother; the future New Jerusalem is pictured as a bride and daughter. A free woman and her bride-to-be daughter. The Holy Spirit and the Church. An elder Jerusalem and a younger Jerusalem. *Like Mother, like daughter.*
:::

:::interpreters
Many commentators read Paul's "mother of us all" as the heavenly city and the new covenant community, the Church, rather than as a Person of the Trinity. Sarah's freedom, in that reading, is the freedom of grace over law.
:::
