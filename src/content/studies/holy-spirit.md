## Who is the Holy Spirit?

The Nicene Creed confesses the Holy Spirit as "the Lord, the giver of life," who "with the Father and the Son is worshipped and glorified, who has spoken through the prophets." Scripture shows the Spirit at work from the first verses of Genesis — "the Spirit of God moved upon the face of the waters" (Genesis 1:2) — to the last invitation of Revelation: "the Spirit and the bride say, Come" (Revelation 22:17).

Yet the Spirit remains the most mysterious Person of the Trinity. Much of what Scripture reveals about the Spirit comes through symbol and allegory — a dove, a wind, fire, water, a lampstand, a tree. *Seven Lamps Before the Throne* was written to gather those images and ask what they reveal together.

## What Scripture says the Spirit does

:::scripture
- **Gives life.** "The Spirit of God hath made me, and the breath of the Almighty hath given me life" (Job 33:4).
- **Gives new birth.** "Except a man be born of water and of the Spirit, he cannot enter into the kingdom of God" (John 3:5).
- **Raised Christ from the dead.** "The Spirit of him that raised up Jesus from the dead dwell in you" (Romans 8:11).
- **Comforts and teaches.** "The Comforter, which is the Holy Ghost… shall teach you all things" (John 14:26).
- **Dwells within believers.** "Ye are the temple of God, and… the Spirit of God dwelleth in you" (1 Corinthians 3:16).
- **Intercedes.** "The Spirit itself maketh intercession for us with groanings which cannot be uttered" (Romans 8:26).
- **Searches the deep things of God** (1 Corinthians 2:10–11) and **bears fruit** in our lives (Galatians 5:22–23).
:::

## The Spirit who gives birth

The heart of Yona Tree's study is the Spirit's work in birth. Jesus told Nicodemus that a man must be "born of water and of the Spirit," and Nicodemus immediately thought of "his mother's womb" (John 3:4–5). The Father begets; the Spirit gives birth; the Son is the only begotten. [Walk through the eleven questions](/articles/who-is-the-holy-spirit-eleven-questions/).

## The Spirit in symbol

The dove at the baptism of Jesus, the hen gathering her brood, the eagle over her nest, the comforting mother of Isaiah 66:13, the wind that blows where it wills, the whirlwind of Ezekiel's vision, the seven lamps before the throne, and the Tree of Life — Yona Tree reads all of these as portraits of one Person. [Explore the dove, the hen and the eagle](/articles/the-dove-the-hen-and-the-eagle/).

## Grammar and identity

English Bibles call the Spirit "He." Greek uses a neuter word for spirit and a masculine word for Comforter; Hebrew and Aramaic use feminine words. Yona Tree's position is that grammar alone does not settle the question either way. [Read The Pronoun Problem](/articles/the-pronoun-problem/).

:::yonatree
The Holy Spirit is our Heavenly Mother who births us into the Family of God; the loving Dove who shelters us under Her wings; the eternal companion of God the Father; Lady Wisdom, the Architect of all creation; our Teacher, our Comforting Helper, the Indweller of our souls and our Intercessor; the Seven Lamps that burn before the Throne of Grace; the Free Woman of Jerusalem above; the Breath of God; the Whirlwind of His divine presence; and our Tree of eternal life.
:::

:::interpreters
Most Christian traditions confess the full deity and personhood of the Holy Spirit while describing the Spirit with masculine pronouns, and many hold that God transcends gender altogether. Throughout church history a minority of writers — in the Syriac tradition, among some early Fathers, and in later movements such as the Moravians — used maternal language for the Spirit. [Read their words](/articles/voices-from-church-history/).
:::

## Search the Scriptures

Whatever you conclude, the Spirit's invitation is the same: to be born again, filled, comforted and led into the truth. "If ye then, being evil, know how to give good gifts unto your children: how much more shall your heavenly Father give the Holy Spirit to them that ask him?" (Luke 11:13).
