## The framework

The righteous awaiting redemption → the redeemed with Christ in the present heaven → resurrected humanity dwelling eternally with God in the new heaven and new earth.

## Stage one — Abraham's bosom

:::scripture
In the account of the rich man and Lazarus, Jesus says that "the beggar died, and was carried by the angels into Abraham's bosom," while the rich man was in torment, and "between us and you there is a great gulf fixed" (Luke 16:22–26).
:::

:::interpreters
Many Christians — following a long tradition reflected in the Apostles' Creed's phrase "he descended into hell" — understand Abraham's bosom as the place of comfort where the righteous of the Old Testament awaited the Redeemer. They connect it with Christ's words to the thief, "To day shalt thou be with me in paradise" (Luke 23:43), and with Paul's statement that Christ "descended first into the lower parts of the earth" and "led captivity captive" (Ephesians 4:8–10). Others read Luke 16 as a parable whose details are not meant to map the afterlife.
:::

## Stage two — the present heavenly paradise

:::scripture
Paul writes that to be "absent from the body" is "to be present with the Lord" (2 Corinthians 5:8), and that to depart is "to be with Christ; which is far better" (Philippians 1:23). He was "caught up into paradise" (2 Corinthians 12:4). Believers have come "unto mount Sion… the heavenly Jerusalem… and to the spirits of just men made perfect" (Hebrews 12:22–23). John saw the souls of the martyrs under the heavenly altar, told to "rest yet for a little season" (Revelation 6:9–11).
:::

:::yonatree
This is the Jerusalem which is above, "the mother of us all" (Galatians 4:26) — the place where redeemed souls find rest after leaving their earthly bodies. As wonderful as it is, Scripture indicates that it is not the final dwelling: the first heaven and earth will pass away (Revelation 21:1).
:::

## Stage three — the new creation and New Jerusalem

:::scripture
At the resurrection the dead are raised incorruptible: "this corruptible must put on incorruption, and this mortal must put on immortality" (1 Corinthians 15:53). "The dead in Christ shall rise first… and so shall we ever be with the Lord" (1 Thessalonians 4:16–17). John saw "a new heaven and a new earth," and the holy city coming down from God: "the tabernacle of God is with men, and he will dwell with them" (Revelation 21:1–3). "They shall see his face" (Revelation 22:4).
:::

:::yonatree
The final home of the righteous is not a disembodied heaven but resurrected life on a renewed creation, where God dwells with humanity in the New Jerusalem, the Bride of the Lamb. What began in a garden ends in a garden-city, with the river and the Tree of Life at its heart.
:::

## Why this matters

The hope of the Christian is not merely to "go to heaven," but to be raised, made new, and to dwell with God forever. "Wherefore comfort one another with these words" (1 Thessalonians 4:18).
