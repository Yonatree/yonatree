## A new heaven and a new earth

> For, behold, I create new heavens and a new earth: and the former shall not be remembered, nor come into mind.
>
> — Isaiah 65:17

Isaiah foresaw it; Peter wrote that "we, according to his promise, look for new heavens and a new earth, wherein dwelleth righteousness" (2 Peter 3:13); and John saw it: "a new heaven and a new earth… and I John saw the holy city, new Jerusalem, coming down from God out of heaven, prepared as a bride adorned for her husband" (Revelation 21:1–2).

## The city in the text

:::scripture
- **God dwells with His people.** "Behold, the tabernacle of God is with men, and he will dwell with them, and they shall be his people" (21:3).
- **No more tears.** "There shall be no more death, neither sorrow, nor crying, neither shall there be any more pain" (21:4).
- **The Bride.** The angel shows John "the bride, the Lamb's wife" — the holy city (21:9–10).
- **Twelve gates.** Three on each side, each a single pearl, with twelve angels and the names of the twelve tribes of Israel (21:12–13, 21).
- **Twelve foundations.** Bearing the names of the twelve apostles of the Lamb, garnished with twelve precious stones (21:14, 19–20).
- **Foursquare.** Twelve thousand furlongs; "the length and the breadth and the height of it are equal" (21:16). The wall is 144 cubits (21:17).
- **Gold like glass.** The city and its street are pure gold, like transparent glass (21:18, 21).
- **No temple, no sun.** The Lord God Almighty and the Lamb are its temple and its light (21:22–23).
- **Open gates.** Never shut by day, "for there shall be no night there" (21:25).
- **The river and the Tree.** The river of the water of life flows from the throne of God and of the Lamb, and the Tree of Life grows on either side (22:1–2).
:::

## Gates and tribes

Ezekiel's vision of a restored city also had twelve gates named for the tribes of Israel, three on each side, and ended with the city's name: "The LORD is there" (Ezekiel 48:31–35). On the high priest's breastplate, twelve precious stones bore the names of the twelve tribes (Exodus 28:17–21) — an echo many readers hear in the twelve foundations.

## The foundations

The foundation stones of Revelation 21:19–20, in order: **jasper, sapphire, chalcedony, emerald, sardonyx, sardius, chrysolite, beryl, topaz, chrysoprasus, jacinth** and **amethyst**. The exact identity of some ancient gem names is uncertain, and the colors shown in the diagram above are approximate.

## Possible interpretations

:::possible
**One people of God.** Tribes on the gates and apostles in the foundations: the saints of both covenants built into one city.
:::

:::possible
**The Holy of Holies.** Solomon's inner sanctuary was a cube — twenty cubits long, wide and high (1 Kings 6:20). A city of equal length, breadth and height may picture the whole city as God's dwelling place, which is why "I saw no temple therein" (21:22).
:::

:::possible
**Eden restored.** The river and the Tree of Life return to the garden, now open rather than guarded.
:::

## Speculation

:::speculation
**Cube or pyramid?** Scripture gives the measurements but does not draw the shape. Christians have pictured the city as a cube, a pyramid, or a symbolic portrait not meant to be drawn at all. Hold such questions loosely.
:::

## The Jerusalem above and the city to come

"Jerusalem which is above is free, which is the mother of us all" (Galatians 4:26). Believers have already "come unto mount Sion… the heavenly Jerusalem" (Hebrews 12:22).

:::yonatree
The present heavenly Jerusalem — where the saints dwell after their lives on earth — is compared to a mother. The New Jerusalem of the new creation is compared to a bride. An elder Jerusalem and a younger Jerusalem: the Holy Spirit and the Church, Mother and daughter. [Read: Three Stages in the Dwelling of the Righteous](/studies/three-dwelling-places/).
:::
